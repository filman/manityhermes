01 — Overview

Built for the environments legacy EHRs were never asked to survive.

Manity Health is a groundbreaking Electronic Health Record and Clinical Data Orchestration Platform built on HL7 FHIR. It is the hub for monitoring and managing the health of individuals operating in high-stress, high-stakes environments — deployed on-premises, decentralized by default, and synchronized peer-to-peer as humans move from vehicle to vehicle.

🛰

Vehicle-agnostic

Subject data follows the spaceflight participant from vehicle to vehicle via libp2p, mDNS peer discovery, and Gossipsub — automatic, selective, and segmented by tag.

🧬

FHIR-native

Every observation, device, specimen, and consent is a first-class FHIR resource — interoperable with the entire US healthcare system out of the box, extensible for the spacecraft.

🔐

Privacy by design

HIPAA, GDPR, PIPEDA. End-to-end encryption, pseudonymization, fine-grained consent via the FHIR Consent Resource, and an OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect identity provider.

🤖

CDS Hooks ready

Standardized integration with internal and external evidence-based and AI/ML decision-support models — predictive DSIs for motion sickness, decompression, hydration, radiation, sleep.

🪐

Decentralized & resilient

Vector clocks plus Conflict-free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs) keep state consistent across partitions, latency, and total comms blackouts. USB sneakernet bulk export when all else fails.

🧩

SMART on FHIR

Bespoke human-factors UIs paired with an App Store of substitutable third-party apps — purpose-built per role and mission, scoped per role and consent.

02 — Architecture

A four-stage pipeline. One canonical data model. Many faces.

Diverse, proprietary, non-standard inputs are normalized into validated FHIR resources via an extensible plugin architecture — buffered, mapped, validated, and committed without coupling ingest concerns to storage or UX.

  1. 01

    Ingestion

    Plugin architecture accepts HL7 FHIR JSON/XML, HL7 v2.x, X12, NCPDP, CCDA, raw sensor time-series at arbitrary frequencies, and raw video. Encrypted buffer for processing.

    FHIR · HL7 v2.x · CCDA · X12 · USCDI · proprietary
  2. 02

    Normalize · Map · Transform

    Mapping repository converts each source format into FHIR resources via standard or custom transformation rules.

    Observation · Device · DeviceMetric · DiagnosticReport
  3. 03

    Validate · Identify · Consent

    FHIR profile compliance check, PII matching to known identities, and consent evaluation. Invalid data and unknown entities are logged for handling.

    FHIR profiles · IdP · Consent Resource · audit
  4. 04

    FHIR Repository

    Validated resources are persistently stored, indexed, searchable, and exposed via RESTful FHIR APIs to authorized SMART on FHIR apps.

    Firely Server · SQL Server 2022 · RESTful FHIR API

Identity Provider

OAuth 2.0 + OpenID Connect. Role-based scopes for patients, researchers, flight control, in-flight medical ops, crew medical officers, and algorithmic users. Least-privilege by default.

Consent Management

FHIR Consent Resource inside the IdP. Patients and research participants get a UI to grant, revoke, and inspect scope. Every consent action is audited as a FHIR AuditEvent.

Decentralization

libp2p for transport, mDNS for peer discovery, Gossipsub for pub/sub. Selective sync via tags — SpacecraftA, G-loads, FlightType, DayInMission.

Data Integrity

Vector clocks track causal order across nodes. CRDTs resolve concurrent updates without central coordination. FHIR versioning + Provenance for chain of custody and rollback.

CDS Hooks

Standardized exchange of clinical and environmental context with external evidence-based and AI/ML knowledge sources — context-aware recommendations within workflows.

Power Optimization

Selective processing on the ingestion pipeline, adaptive sync frequency tuned to network conditions, data compression — engineered for resource-constrained environments.

03 — Capabilities

Five questions TRISH asked. Five concrete answers.

Direct excerpts from Section B of the Manity HERMES submission — how the platform behaves under the conditions HERMES is designed to test.

Q1 Ingesting non-standard, never-before-seen device data

For a hypothetical proprietary cabin-humidity sensor streaming XML time-series at 1 Hz over TCP/IP LLP, Manity develops a tailored ingestion plugin. Raw data is buffered, then walked through the four-stage pipeline — mapped to FHIR via the data-mapping library, validated against FHIR profiles, associated to identity and consent, and stored as searchable FHIR resources behind the RESTful FHIR API.

Out of the box, Manity ships connectors for HL7 FHIR, HL7 v2.x, CCDA v1/v2, USCDI, and X12.

Q2 International participants & cross-jurisdictional privacy

Privacy-by-Design baked into the core: data minimization, PII pseudonymization, right to access, right to erasure, segmentation, and data portability. Data may be segmented per subject jurisdictional authority. DPAs with third-party processors; DPIAs to surface and mitigate risk.

FHIR's global terminology and regional adaptations enable integration of data from diverse EHRs and HIEs. The IdP enforces fine-grained, permission-based access via OAuth 2.0 + OIDC. Consent is managed in a dedicated layer, allowing participants to track and revise privacy consents as required by GDPR and other regional regulations.

Q3 Following the participant from vehicle to vehicle

Peer-to-peer sync via libp2p, mDNS, and Gossipsub. Vector clocks track causal order; CRDTs resolve concurrent updates; FHIR resources are extended with the metadata both require. When the participant boards a new vehicle, the new instance discovers the previous instance via mDNS and synchronizes via Gossipsub.

Tagging — patient-data, mission-data, research-data, environment-specific tags — enables selective sync. The new vehicle pulls only the participant's health data, leaving mission and research data on the prior peer.

Q4 Autonomous comparison against preloaded baselines

Preflight and prior-vehicle data is ingested as FHIR via the same pipeline. The FHIR Provenance resource captures origin and history; tags by phase, vehicle, and mission stage make it queryable. ML and CDS systems integrate via CDS Hooks with full clinical and environmental context.

Limitations explicitly named:

  • Data quality of the source feeds
  • Latency, timeliness, and power constraints in-flight
  • Interpretability of complex ML/CDS output
  • Algorithmic bias on small spacefaring sample sizes
  • Translational research gaps in space-environment health
Q5 Vehicle environmental data — O₂, CO₂, vibration, radiation, water

Subject-attributable measurements (ambient O₂, CO₂, temperature, humidity, radiation exposure) flow into FHIR Observation resources scoped to the participant. Vehicle-general measurements (vibration, gravitational loads, toxin detection, surface contaminants, water quality) flow into FHIR Device and DeviceMetric resources, linked to mission context via custom extensions and tags.

Both shapes coexist in the same FHIR repository — allowing real-time correlation of environment to subject without losing either lens.
